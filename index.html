Unmanned Systems Lab

We work on learning, teaming, and control for unmanned aircraft that operate alongside human partners. The lab designs its own airframes, flight control boards, software, AI/ML algorithms, and human–AI teaming strategies, and tests them outdoors so that results hold up in real-world deployment.

Research

Research directions

Full research description →
01

Human-guided learning and human–AI teaming

We build autonomy that learns from people. Our methods learn rewards from human ratings and preferences instead of hand-designed objectives, and model teammates and opponents so an agent can coordinate with partners it has never trained with.

02

Fair, interpretable, and aligned reinforcement learning

We develop reinforcement learning that is fair across objectives and agents, interpretable through symbolic policy distillation and model explanations, and aligned at deployment through inference-time policy alignment and adaptation of vision-language-action models.

03

Autonomy, guidance, control, and multi-agent coordination

The lab has a long record in distributed coordination, consensus, containment, and event-triggered control. Current work is in nonlinear guidance for target enclosing and interception, safety-critical guidance under input constraints, and flexible leader–follower formation of fixed-wing aircraft.

04

Deployment and technology transition

We focus on deployment of learning, autonomy, and control models and algorithms on hardware in real-world environments. We build our own small aircraft, from airframe and flight control board to software, and work with sponsors to carry results into practice.

Director

Yongcan Cao

Professor and Chair of the Department of Electrical Engineering at UTSA, and holder of the Mary Lou Clarke Endowed Distinguished Professorship. His research is in robotics, autonomy, artificial intelligence, multi-agent systems, and human–robot interaction.

He chairs the IEEE CSS Technical Committee on Manufacturing Automation and Robotic Control, and serves as Associate Editor for Automatica and IEEE Transactions on Cybernetics. He is an Associate Fellow of AIAA, a Microsoft President’s Endowed Fellow, an AFOSR Summer Faculty Fellow, and a recipient of the National Research Council Research Associateship Award at AFRL.
